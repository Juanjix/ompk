# BRAND BIBLE v0.1

Buenos Aires, Argentina
1967 — 1985 / Reinterpreted Today

## 00. BRAND DEFINITION

OMPK is an independent Argentine clothing label born from the intersection of:
music, fashion, graphic design, photography and Buenos Aires counterculture.

The brand takes inspiration from the cultural revolution that happened in Buenos Aires between the late 1960s and mid-1980s and translates that energy into contemporary clothing, objects and experiences.

We do not reproduce the past.
We use its language to create something new.

OMPK is not a vintage clothing brand.
OMPK is not rock merchandise.
OMPK is not an Argentine souvenir brand.
OMPK is a contemporary cultural label built in Buenos Aires.

## 01. THE CENTRAL IDEA

BUENOS AIRES IS THE MAIN CHARACTER.

There is no single icon behind the brand.
Not Spinetta. Not Charly. Not Pappo. Not Cerati.
The mythology belongs to the city itself.

Buenos Aires at different moments:
- 1967 — experimentation
- 1970 — a new generation
- 1973 — freedom and excess
- 1976 — underground culture
- 1982 — electricity
- 1985 — the new city

Each collection can enter a different moment of this universe. The city becomes an infinite archive.

## 02. BRAND PURPOSE

Create clothing and cultural objects for a new generation using the creative spirit of Buenos Aires as raw material.

The objective is not nostalgia. The objective is continuity.

To connect: PAST → PRESENT → NEXT GENERATION

## 03. BRAND PHILOSOPHY

THE PAST IS MATERIAL, NOT DESTINATION.

The brand should never look like costume. We don't want someone to look like they are dressed for a 1973 themed party. We want them to look contemporary while carrying something from that world.

## 04. BRAND MANIFESTO

Buenos Aires was never quiet.

Behind apartment doors, inside recording studios, underground bars, theatres, rehearsal rooms and printing workshops, something was always happening.

Records were being made. Magazines were being printed. Posters were being pasted. Songs were being written. People were looking for another way to dress, think and live.

OMPK comes from that energy. Not from nostalgia for another time. From the belief that culture moves forward when someone decides to make something that wasn't there before.

We make clothes. But clothes are only part of the story.

Music matters. Photography matters. Books matter. Design matters. The places where people meet matter. The night matters. Buenos Aires matters.

We look backwards only to find new ways forward.

Made in Buenos Aires.

## 05. BRAND POSITIONING

**Category**: Independent fashion / cultural label.

**Positioning**: Premium-accessible independent clothing. Not luxury. Not fast fashion. Not streetwear. Not traditional heritage.

The ideal space is somewhere between: fashion label × record label × cultural magazine.

## 06. THE CULTURAL TERRITORY

Primary historical territory: **BUENOS AIRES / 1967—1985** — but references should not be treated literally.

**Music**: Argentine rock, psychedelia, folk, blues, progressive rock, glam, post-punk, new wave

**Design**: Instituto Di Tella, Argentine editorial design, record sleeves, concert posters, industrial advertising, underground publishing

**Press**: Pelo, Pinap, Expreso Imaginario, Mordisco, independent publications

**Culture**: record stores, recording studios, theatres, cafés, bars, apartments, nightlife, independent publishing, art galleries, cinema

## 07. CREATIVE FORMULA

The brand exists at the intersection of four visual worlds:

**01 — Modernism**: Di Tella, grid, grotesk typography, whitespace, geometry, information, discipline

**02 — Rock culture**: records, concerts, studios, amplifiers, photography, posters, backstage, youth

**03 — Editorial culture**: magazines, newspapers, columns, captions, issue numbers, dates, archives, photography

**04 — Psychedelia**: hand lettering, illustration, unexpected colour, organic shapes, surrealism, imperfection

Approximate visual balance:
- 40% Modernism
- 30% Editorial
- 20% Rock
- 10% Psychedelia

Psychedelia is seasoning. Never the entire meal.

## 08. BRAND PERSONALITY

OMPK is:

- **Independent** — doesn't follow fashion calendars blindly
- **Cultured** — knows music, art, photography and design, but never tries to demonstrate how intelligent it is
- **Romantic** — there is emotion behind the objects
- **Rebellious** — but never juvenile
- **Nocturnal** — the city after dark is part of the mythology
- **Sensual** — through fabric, photography and silhouette; not explicit sexuality
- **Imperfect** — human imperfections are desirable
- **Curious** — constantly discovers music, places and people

## 09. WHAT THE BRAND IS NOT

Mandatory for every designer, photographer, developer and AI working on the brand.

OMPK IS NOT:
- ❌ Argentine souvenir design
- ❌ tango cliché
- ❌ football imagery
- ❌ flags everywhere
- ❌ Obelisco merchandising
- ❌ mate iconography
- ❌ rock-band merchandise
- ❌ hippie costume
- ❌ American western cosplay
- ❌ generic 70s nostalgia
- ❌ Instagram vintage filter aesthetic
- ❌ luxury minimalism
- ❌ hype streetwear
- ❌ fake distressed graphics everywhere
- ❌ Good Morning Keith Argentina

The references may have started there. The resulting identity must stand independently.

## 10. VISUAL PRINCIPLE

**ORDER + CHAOS**

This is one of the most important rules of the identity.

The base design should feel: structured, modernist, rational, editorial.

Then one element breaks the system. For example:
- Perfect grid + handwritten title
- Helvetica + psychedelic illustration
- Clean photograph + registration error
- Minimal product page + huge archival typography
- White page + acid green element

The tension creates identity.

## 11. LOGO SYSTEM

**Update — decided departure from the "not vintage" rule below.** The original rule in this section said the logo should not initially attempt to look vintage. After testing a bold, connected script (instrument-headstock energy, in the spirit of a Rickenbacker logo without tracing it) against the grotesk Ring-O direction, the founder chose the script as the primary wordmark — knowingly, not by accident. Logged here so the reasoning survives: the script does more of the brand's actual emotional work (rock/instrument culture, §07) than a safe grotesk did, and the risk it names (reading as costume) is managed by keeping everything *around* the wordmark — photography, product, copy — contemporary, per §03.

The primary wordmark should be: simple, confident, typographic, recognizable, highly reproducible. *(Now read "confident" as "confident even as a script" — legibility and reproducibility still apply; "not vintage" no longer does, by choice.)*

**Primary wordmark**: OMPK, set in a bold, connected script (production reference: Yellowtail as the placeholder web font; a custom-drawn version should replace it before launch — see `docs/logo-system-v01.md`). OMPK is Oliver Martin Peralta Kennedy's initials; kept internal unless/until the brand chooses to tell that story publicly.

**Functional fallback**: at sizes where the script loses legibility (favicon, browser tab, fine print), use plain-set "OMPK" in Archivo — utility only, not a competing mark.

**Secondary mark**: a 2×2 grid monogram seal (O M / P K), block-lettered (not script — needs to stay legible embossed/engraved at small scale), reserved for hardware, leather emboss and interior stamps. See `docs/logo-system-v01.md`.

**Location lockup**:

**Archive lockup**:

**Catalog system**:

## 12. TYPOGRAPHY

Three voices:

**Type A — Information** (neutral grotesk)
Reference territory: Helvetica, Neue Haas Grotesk, Univers, Akzidenz Grotesk
Used for: navigation, prices, sizes, labels, product data, dates, catalog numbers, captions
Characteristics: precise, neutral, functional

**Type B — Editorial** (serif)
Reference territory: Times, Plantin, Caslon, Bookman
Used for: stories, manifestos, editorial introductions, quotes, long descriptions
Characteristics: literary, warm, historical

**Type C — Cultural** (custom lettering, used sparingly)
Inspired by: concert posters, record sleeves, hand-painted signs, Argentine psychedelic graphics
Used for: collections, T-shirts, posters, campaign titles, special releases
Never use Type C for interface/navigation.

## 13. COLOR SYSTEM

**Core**
- Paper — `#E8E0CE` (primary background, not pure white)
- Ink — `#171714` (primary black, slightly warmer than digital black)
- Newsprint — `#C9C1AE` (secondary neutral)

**Signal colors**
- Signal Red — `#C83A2B`
- Industrial Yellow — `#E2B52B`
- Pop Green — `#477343`

**Cultural colors**
- Tobacco — `#7A4A32`
- Wine — `#6A3236`
- Acid Green — `#8C9C38`
- Burnt Orange — `#C8642D`

## 14. COLOR RULE

Never use every colour simultaneously.

Most compositions should use: Paper + Ink + ONE signal color.

The identity should remain restrained enough that unusual colour becomes meaningful.

## 15. PHOTOGRAPHY

Photography is not supporting material. Photography is part of the product.

**Format**: 35mm feeling. Not fake Instagram film. Real texture whenever possible.

Characteristics: film grain, natural skin, slight underexposure, direct flash, motion blur, deep shadows, unexpected cropping, available light, imperfect focus.

## 16. TWO PHOTOGRAPHIC MODES

**Mode A — Portrait**
Inspired by Argentine documentary/art photography. Black & white. Strong composition. Architecture. Silence. Direct gaze. Minimal posing.

**Mode B — Night**
Bars, apartments, recording studios, street, cars, backstage, restaurants, hotels. Flash. Movement. Smoke/haze where naturally present. Friends. Music. Life.

The campaign should feel like someone was actually there. Not like a production pretending to be there.

## 17. LOCATIONS

**Preferred**: old Buenos Aires apartments, recording studios, record stores, neighbourhood clubs, bars, cafés, rationalist buildings, old hotels, theatres, terraces, rehearsal rooms, workshops, printing shops, city streets at night.

**Avoid**: generic photo studios, fake American diners, generic desert imagery, fake cowboy environments, obvious tourist landmarks.

Buenos Aires should be felt rather than shown.

## 18. INTERIOR LANGUAGE

**Materials**: wood, chrome, glass, leather, vinyl, paper, fabric, analogue electronics

**Objects**: records, turntables, speakers, reel-to-reel machines, microphones, amplifiers, magazines, ashtrays, lamps, books, cameras, contact sheets, posters

Objects should feel lived-in. Never arranged like props in a theme park.

## 19. TEXTURE SYSTEM

Digital perfection is undesirable.

**Approved textures**: paper grain, newsprint, halftone, ink bleed, xerox noise, screen-print imperfections, film grain, dust, fold marks, registration errors, tape, rubber stamps, handwriting, printer marks.

Texture should support information, never destroy it.

## 20. CLOTHING

The first collection should be small. We prefer fewer recognizable pieces over a large generic catalogue.

**Update — the trouser is the hero garment, not the tee.** Fashion is the product; music is the cultural code. The brand should become recognizable partly through its trousers before its graphics. Everything else in the collection is built to support that one silhouette.

**Core garments**:
1. Oxford / Flare / Bootcut Trouser — **hero garment**
2. Ringer Tee
3. Natural Tee
4. Raglan
5. 70s Collar Shirt
6. Denim Jacket

**Potential expansion**: leather, knitwear, tailoring, boots, scarves, bags

**The permanent trouser family** — a standing, numbered line the brand keeps in the range across seasons, the way a denim label keeps a fit number alive for decades:
- BOOTCUT Nº01
- FLARE Nº02
- VELVET Nº03
- DENIM Nº04

Names above are placeholders for the fit numbers, not final product names — see Section 22.

## 21. SILHOUETTE

References 1967–1985 without reproducing costume.

Key concepts: slightly fitted tees, high-waisted trousers, flare, bootcut, cropped jackets, wide collars, shorter jackets, unisex cuts, fluid shirts.

**Hero trouser — fit reference.** Mid-to-high rise, five-pocket construction with pewter or aged-silver hardware (rivets, jean button, no branded stamping visible from a distance), straight through the thigh with the flare beginning at or just below the knee, break landing exactly at the top of a boot — never pooling, never cropped. The fit should read as tailoring wearing denim's construction, not denim trying to look tailored.

Colorway range for the hero trouser: raw indigo denim, black sateen/twill, black corduroy, white denim, fine cotton pinstripe, and striped hickory (railroad ticking) — one silhouette, six surfaces, so the fit stays recognizable regardless of fabric.

*Internal reference benchmark (fit and colorway range only, not a creative direction to imitate): GMK's Mick Flare Jeans line — noted here as a market comparable for how a flare/bootcut fit and hardware read in product photography, not as a source to copy.*

The garments should work independently. Someone wearing only one OMPK piece should still look contemporary.

## 22. PRODUCT NAMING

Never: "Black Oversized T-Shirt"

Products should belong to the universe. Potential naming structures:

**Places**: Florida, Corrientes, Balvanera, San Telmo, Rivadavia

**Time**: 1967, After Midnight, Sunday Morning, 03:17 AM

**Cultural language**: Studio, Issue, Session, Archive, Signal, Frequency

Examples: Florida Ringer, Rivadavia Flare, Studio 01 Shirt, After Midnight Tee, Signal Jacket, Issue Nº1 Raglan

Avoid directly naming products after famous musicians without rights/clear reason.

## 23. COLLECTION SYSTEM

Collections should feel like records or cultural events. Not "SPRING / SUMMER 2027" — instead:

```
COLLECTION 001
AFTER MIDNIGHT
Buenos Aires / 1973—2027
```

Each collection receives: visual language, photography, playlist, story, printed matter, catalogue number.

## 24. CATALOG SYSTEM

Every product can receive an archival code, e.g. `BRAND-001-TEE`, `BRAND-002-RNG`, `BRAND-003-FLR`.

Customer-facing: `CAT. Nº 001`

Appears on: labels, packaging, website, receipts, campaigns, garments.

Makes each object feel catalogued rather than merely stocked.

## 25. PACKAGING

Should feel like buying a record or independent magazine.

Elements: recycled paper, cream cardboard, black ink, one signal colour, catalogue number, stamp, folded editorial sheet, playlist card, sticker.

Every order should contain something worth keeping.

## 26. HANG TAG

Not a luxury-fashion tag. Think: museum archive, record label, library card, concert ticket.

```
OMPK
CAT. Nº 001
GARMENT: RINGER TEE
COLOR: PAPER / INK
MADE IN: BUENOS AIRES
EDITION: 01
```

## 27. TONE OF VOICE

The brand speaks calmly. It does not scream. It does not try to be cool. It assumes the audience is intelligent.

Voice: short, observational, cultural, slightly poetic, confident, understated.

## 28. COPY RULES

**Avoid**: "🔥 NEW DROP 🔥", "Must-have", "Elevate your style", "Premium quality", "Timeless pieces", "Rock your look". Generic fashion copy is forbidden.

**Prefer**:
- "Buenos Aires. 03:17 AM."
- "Some things sound better after midnight."
- "Made slowly. Played loud."
- "CAT. Nº 004 / BUENOS AIRES"
- "A shirt for staying out longer than planned."

The copy creates a world. It doesn't explain one.

## 29. LANGUAGE

Primary communication: Spanish + English selectively. Spanish establishes origin. English can appear naturally through musical/fashion vocabulary.

Do not translate everything. Do not artificially anglicize the brand. Buenos Aires is an advantage, not something to hide.

## 30. DIGITAL EXPERIENCE

The website should feel between: fashion store, record label, independent magazine, archive.

Primary navigation: SHOP / COLLECTIONS / STORIES / RADIO / ARCHIVE / ABOUT

## 31. HOMEPAGE

Should NOT open like a traditional ecommerce store.

**Avoid**: SALE, FREE SHIPPING, SHOP NOW, product grid immediately.

**Instead**: Full-screen campaign image/video →
```
OMPK
BUENOS AIRES
COLLECTION 001
AFTER MIDNIGHT
```
Then: EDITORIAL → PRODUCT → CULTURE → ARCHIVE

Commerce exists inside the world. The world does not exist around commerce.

## 32. SHOP

Brutally simple. Large photographs. Product name. Catalogue number. Price. Minimal UI.

No unnecessary cards. No rounded SaaS components. No gradients. No ecommerce visual noise.

## 33. PRODUCT PAGE

Combines commerce and editorial:

PRODUCT NAME → CAT. Nº → PRICE → PHOTOGRAPHY → STORY → MATERIAL → FIT → SIZE → ORIGIN → CARE → ARCHIVE REFERENCE → RELATED RADIO SESSION

Buying a shirt should also reveal where the idea came from.

## 34. RADIO

Radio is a first-class brand property. Not a Spotify widget hidden in the footer.

```
OMPK RADIO
RADIO Nº001 — Buenos Aires 1973
RADIO Nº002 — After Midnight
RADIO Nº003 — Electric City
```

Each session contains: cover, short text, tracklist, playlist link, date, catalogue number.

Radio allows music to remain central without turning musicians into merchandise.

## 35. STORIES

Editorial content about: music, photography, design, architecture, people, places, objects, records, fashion.

Stories do not need to sell a product. This is essential. Culture builds the audience. Commerce monetizes part of that relationship.

## 36. ARCHIVE

One of the strongest differentiators. Can contain: past collections, campaigns, radio sessions, photography, posters, editorials, objects, research.

Eventually the website becomes a cultural repository.

## 37. SOCIAL MEDIA

Instagram should not look like a product catalogue.

Ideal ratio: 30% clothing / 25% photography / 20% archive/culture / 15% music / 10% process

Objective: someone should want to follow OMPK even if they never buy clothing.

## 38. ART DIRECTION RULE

For every piece of communication ask: **Would this still be interesting if the logo disappeared?**

If no: the creative idea isn't strong enough.

## 39. PRODUCT RULE

For every garment ask: **Would someone wear this without knowing the reference?**

If no: it is merchandise. Redesign it.

## 40. CULTURAL RULE

For every historical reference ask: **Are we interpreting this or simply reproducing it?**

If reproducing: stop. The archive is inspiration, not inventory.

## 41. LEGAL / CREATIVE BOUNDARY

Do not build commercial graphics around unauthorized: artist portraits, album artwork, band logos, song lyrics, magazine covers, photographs, trademarks.

References should inform new work rather than be reproduced. Original photography and original graphic design are preferred. Historical material belongs primarily in properly sourced editorial/archive contexts where legally appropriate.

## 42. SUSTAINABILITY POSITION

Do not invent environmental credentials.

Initial truthful territory: SMALL BATCHES, LOCAL PRODUCTION, LIMITED RUNS, MADE IN ARGENTINA, LONG-LIFE GARMENTS.

If deadstock or certified fabrics are later introduced, communicate them specifically. Never use vague greenwashing language.

Introduces the city at night. Not historical reconstruction — contemporary Buenos Aires seen through the visual memory of 1973–1985.

**Locations**: apartment, bar, recording studio, street, taxi, terrace

**Products**:
1. After Midnight Tee
2. Florida Ringer
3. Studio Raglan
4. Rivadavia Flare
5. Session Shirt
6. Signal Denim

**Palette**: Paper, Ink, Wine, Tobacco, Signal Red

## 44. COLLECTION Nº001 CAMPAIGN

Narrative: Two or three people leave an apartment. They go somewhere. We never completely understand where. Street. Taxi. Bar. Studio. Another apartment. Sunrise.

The clothing appears naturally. No traditional modelling. No product pointing. No fake rock-band performance.

It should feel like fragments of a night someone actually lived.

## 45. BRAND EXPERIENCE

OMPK should feel like discovering: a record you didn't know, a photograph you've never seen, a magazine from another era, a bar someone recommended, and a piece of clothing you want to keep for years.

All inside the same universe.

## 46. BRAND EQUATION

BUENOS AIRES × MUSIC × DESIGN × NIGHT × CLOTHING = OMPK

## 47. INTERNAL CREATIVE TEST

Before approving anything, evaluate against five questions:

1. Does it feel Argentine without using clichés?
2. Does it feel informed by history without looking like costume?
3. Would it work without the logo?
4. Could this belong to a cultural institution as easily as a fashion label?
5. Would we still find this interesting ten years from now?

If fewer than four answers are YES: rework it.

## 48. NORTH STAR

We are not creating the Argentine Good Morning Keith.

We are creating: **A CULTURAL LABEL FROM BUENOS AIRES.**

Clothing is the first medium. Not necessarily the last.

## 49. FINAL PRINCIPLE

Don't recreate 1973.
Create what 1973 would have become.
