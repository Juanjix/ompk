# PHASE 02B — Naming Round 02 / Fashion First

Status: exploration only. `[BRAND]` in `docs/BRAND_BIBLE.md` is untouched. No logo, no typography, no site work. Round 01 (`Phase 02` conversation / published artifact) is preserved as research, not deleted.

## 01 — Strategic clarification carried into this round

> Fashion is the product. Music is the cultural code. Buenos Aires is the origin. Archive and editorial are the storytelling system.

Round 01 over-indexed on archive / record-label / independent-press language. This round tests every name against a harder question: **would this look desirable embroidered inside the waistband of an exceptional pair of bootcut trousers** — and could a musician wear that pair on stage while someone else wears the identical pair to a bar or gallery, without either one looking costumed.

The trouser (bootcut / flare / Oxford) is the hero garment going forward. The T-shirt is not the defining object.

## 04 — Shortlist of 12 (deep analysis)

Selected for the strongest combination of fashion credibility, musical attitude and international reach, pulled across six of the eight territories.

### Ámbar (F)
- **As fashion:** Already sounds like a material — warm resin, amber leather, amber light. Premium without trying.
- **Music inside it:** Indirect — the amber glow of a stage light, a bar at closing time. Felt, not stated.
- **Personality:** Warm, confident, unhurried.
- **Weakness:** Slight risk of reading as a jewelry or perfume name before a clothing one.
- **Pronunciation:** Trivial everywhere — "amber" is recognizable cold in English, French, German.
- **On the hero trouser:** Excellent — short, warm, sits well on a small woven label.
- **Requires explanation?** No.
- **Who wears it:** Someone who dresses like the party already started without them trying.

### Ruggeri (A)
- **As fashion:** Reads instantly as a designer surname — the "-eri" ending already does the work Missoni and Ferragamo do.
- **Music inside it:** None literal — the music comes from imagining who "Ruggeri" is, not what they play.
- **Personality:** Confident, a little aloof, old-money-adjacent without actually being old money.
- **Weakness:** Being a plausible real surname means eventual name-conflict checks matter more here than elsewhere.
- **Pronunciation:** Easy in Italian, Spanish, English; a genuine international surname shape.
- **On the hero trouser:** Very strong — reads like an actual label signature.
- **Requires explanation?** No — and that's the point; it invites "who's Ruggeri?" rather than needing an answer upfront.
- **Who wears it:** Someone who looks like they inherited good taste rather than bought it.

### Encore (H)
- **As fashion:** A word fashion hasn't claimed yet, unlike most music words.
- **Music inside it:** Directly the brief's own image — the clothes worn walking back onstage for one more song.
- **Personality:** Generous, a little theatrical, unwilling to let the night end on schedule.
- **Weakness:** Says "music" more plainly than any other finalist-tier name — less mystery than Ámbar or Ruggeri.
- **Pronunciation:** Effectively identical in English and French already; zero friction globally.
- **On the hero trouser:** Strong, though it types slightly more "event" than "garment."
- **Requires explanation?** No.
- **Who wears it:** The person who isn't ready to leave, and isn't apologizing for it.

### Zurdo (F)
- **As fashion:** Short, hard-consonant, already sounds like a streetwear-adjacent premium label.
- **Music inside it:** The left-handed player who does everything differently and still looks right doing it.
- **Personality:** Contrarian, unbothered, quietly rebellious.
- **Weakness:** In football contexts specifically, "zurdo" reads as a compliment about a left foot — a small association to manage, not a disqualifier.
- **Pronunciation:** The "z" softens to "s" in Latin America, harder in Spain — minor but real inconsistency.
- **On the hero trouser:** Excellent — four letters, strong shape, ages well.
- **Requires explanation?** Barely.
- **Who wears it:** Someone who does things their own way and never announces it.

### Olaia (F)
- **As fashion:** Soft, vowel-forward, sits comfortably next to real fashion names without feeling try-hard.
- **Music inside it:** None directly — carries mood rather than reference.
- **Personality:** Elegant, a little mysterious, unforced.
- **Weakness:** Closest of the twelve to "beautiful but blank" — will need the brand to do real work giving it meaning.
- **Pronunciation:** Clean in every language tested — no hard consonant clusters anywhere.
- **On the hero trouser:** Strong, feminine-leaning but not exclusively so.
- **Requires explanation?** Yes, more than most — this is the one where "meaning built after" (Section 06/F) is doing the heaviest lifting.
- **Who wears it:** Someone whose whole look nobody can quite place the reference for.

### Velur (F)
- **As fashion:** Reads like a fabric before it reads like a word — velvet's shadow without spelling velvet.
- **Music inside it:** Tactile, sensory, closer to touch than to sound — the texture of a stage curtain, a jacket lining.
- **Personality:** Sensual, plush, a little decadent.
- **Weakness:** Close enough to "velour" that it may read as literally naming a fabric rather than a brand.
- **Pronunciation:** Reads naturally in Spanish, French and English alike.
- **On the hero trouser:** Strong, especially if the label ever runs an actual velvet piece.
- **Requires explanation?** Minimal.
- **Who wears it:** Someone who dresses for how the clothes feel, not just how they look.

### Azor (F)
- **As fashion:** Sharp, short, looks strong printed or woven — a hawk's shape without the literal bird.
- **Music inside it:** None — pure sound and attitude, closer to a stage name than a lyric.
- **Personality:** Sharp-eyed, fast, a little dangerous.
- **Weakness:** No built-in story; entirely dependent on the brand assigning it meaning.
- **Pronunciation:** Clean everywhere, two syllables, hard "z" is a feature not a bug.
- **On the hero trouser:** Very strong typographically — few letters, high contrast shapes.
- **Requires explanation?** Yes, by design (Territory F rule).
- **Who wears it:** Someone who moves through a room quickly and is remembered after they've left it.

### Lento Incendio (B)
- **As fashion:** The best "said out loud" moment of the two-word set — genuinely sounds desirable.
- **Music inside it:** A slow-burning song, not a fast one — the mood of the last set of the night.
- **Personality:** Smoldering, patient, confident it doesn't need to rush.
- **Weakness:** Two words is a real handicap for a logo, a hashtag, an Instagram handle.
- **Pronunciation:** Easy for Spanish speakers; "incendio" is close enough to "incendiary" to travel.
- **On the hero trouser:** Would likely need to compress — "L.I." or a single-word extraction over time.
- **Requires explanation?** No — it explains itself.
- **Who wears it:** Someone who takes their time and clearly doesn't need to prove anything.

### Canchero (G)
- **As fashion:** Genuinely distinctive — no other city's slang produces this exact word.
- **Music inside it:** The musician who makes something hard look accidental.
- **Personality:** Effortless, a little cocky, likeable rather than arrogant.
- **Weakness:** Lowest international-travel score of the twelve — needs the brand to teach the word.
- **Pronunciation:** Fine for Spanish speakers, genuinely unfamiliar shape for most others.
- **On the hero trouser:** Strong domestically, a bigger bet internationally.
- **Requires explanation?** Yes, and the payoff is good once explained.
- **Who wears it:** The person in the room who isn't trying and still looks the best.

### Humo Y Neón (E)
- **As fashion:** Cinematic rather than descriptive — a scene, not a slogan.
- **Music inside it:** The exact texture of the brand's own Mode B photography — haze, light, motion.
- **Personality:** Atmospheric, a little dangerous, romantic.
- **Weakness:** Two words, and "neón" leans slightly more nightlife-branding than the brief wants.
- **Pronunciation:** Both words travel — "humo" less so than "neón," which is nearly universal.
- **On the hero trouser:** Would need real typographic confidence to avoid reading like a bar sign.
- **Requires explanation?** Minimal.
- **Who wears it:** Someone who's always leaving one place for another, unbothered by which.

### A Ninguna Parte (E)
- **As fashion:** The most quietly poetic of the two/three-word candidates — restrained rather than dramatic.
- **Music inside it:** The taxi ride after the set, going nowhere in particular on purpose.
- **Personality:** Aimless by choice, unbothered, quietly romantic.
- **Weakness:** Three words is heavy for a mark; would likely compress to "Ninguna Parte" or an initialism over time.
- **Pronunciation:** Manageable for non-Spanish speakers with light coaching; not instant.
- **On the hero trouser:** Would need a compressed lockup to work at small scale.
- **Requires explanation?** Slightly, but rewards the read.
- **Who wears it:** Someone who left without saying where they were going.

### Desafinado (H)
- **As fashion:** Unusual choice — a word about being technically wrong that reads as confidently right.
- **Music inside it:** The most literal musical reference in the twelve, but about feel, not gear.
- **Personality:** Human, imperfect on purpose, unbothered by precision.
- **Weakness:** Faint echo of an existing well-known song title in the same word — worth a clearance check, not necessarily disqualifying.
- **Pronunciation:** Five syllables, manageable but not instant for non-Spanish speakers.
- **On the hero trouser:** Reads well typographically, long-ish for a small woven label.
- **Requires explanation?** Some — the "celebrates imperfection" angle needs one sentence to land.
- **Who wears it:** Someone who'd rather be interesting than correct.

## 05 — Twelve vs. Round 01 survivors

Set side by side, the round-01 survivors (Ultramar, Vaho, Acetato, Sereno, Atorrante) are more *finished* — each carries a real, checkable piece of history that a hang tag can lean on for a decade. The round-02 twelve are more *alive* — several of them (Ámbar, Ruggeri, Zurdo, Azor) sound like they could be a fashion label tomorrow with zero explanation required, which is exactly what Section 13 asked this round to prioritize over cleverness. Neither pool wins outright: the finalist set below deliberately draws from both, because "sounds like fashion" and "carries real depth" are not actually in conflict — they're just answering different halves of the brief.

## 06 — Five finalists (no winner selected)

### Ámbar

```
WAISTBAND                     COLLECTION                    GARMENT
ÁMBAR                         ÁMBAR                         ÁMBAR
BUENOS AIRES                  COLLECTION Nº001               CAT. Nº 001
BOOTCUT Nº01                  AFTER MIDNIGHT                 MADE IN ARGENTINA

SPOKEN — "These are Ámbar."
```
Amber isn't a reference to anything — it's a color and a warmth that already exists in the brand's own palette (tobacco, wine, signal red all sit near it), which means the name doesn't need to be taught, only recognized. Its real potential is becoming the word for a specific hour of the night, the way "golden hour" became one for a time of day — once the brand owns that hour, Ámbar stops being a gemstone and starts being a feeling people reach for on purpose.

### Ruggeri

```
WAISTBAND                     COLLECTION                    GARMENT
RUGGERI                       RUGGERI                       RUGGERI
BUENOS AIRES                  COLLECTION Nº001               CAT. Nº 001
BOOTCUT Nº01                  AFTER MIDNIGHT                 MADE IN ARGENTINA

SPOKEN — "These are Ruggeri."
```
A surname carries a hidden promise most invented names can't: that there's a person behind it, whether or not one is ever named. Fashion has built entire houses on exactly that ambiguity — the public never needing to know if "Ruggeri" is a real designer, a grandfather, or nobody at all, only that the name sounds like it has always been there. Given ten years, it could plausibly outgrow being "an Argentine label" entirely and just be a name people know, the way nobody asks if Missoni is still a family anymore.

### Ultramar

```
WAISTBAND                     COLLECTION                    GARMENT
ULTRAMAR                      ULTRAMAR                       ULTRAMAR
BUENOS AIRES                  COLLECTION Nº001               CAT. Nº 001
BOOTCUT Nº01                  AFTER MIDNIGHT                 MADE IN ARGENTINA

SPOKEN — "These are Ultramar."
```
Carried over from Round 01 because the fashion-first lens made it stronger, not weaker: a port city's whole identity — everyone here arrived across a sea — compressed into one internationally legible word, backed by a real pigment history a hang tag can cite without it ever going stale. Its ceiling is higher than a merely pretty word because it can hold an actual color (a deep blue) as a permanent house signature the way other name-driven brands never manage to.

### Zurdo

```
WAISTBAND                     COLLECTION                    GARMENT
ZURDO                         ZURDO                          ZURDO
BUENOS AIRES                  COLLECTION Nº001               AFTER MIDNIGHT
BOOTCUT Nº01                  AFTER MIDNIGHT                 MADE IN ARGENTINA

SPOKEN — "These are Zurdo."
```
Four letters, hard shape, and a built-in idea — doing things against the grain and looking right doing it — that fashion brands normally have to invent from scratch through years of campaigns. Its ceiling is cultural rather than just aesthetic: "zurdo" already carries a small, warm mythology in Spanish (the left-handed guitar player, the left-footed star) that the brand doesn't have to build, only borrow the shape of and make its own.

### Encore

```
WAISTBAND                     COLLECTION                    GARMENT
ENCORE                        ENCORE                         ENCORE
BUENOS AIRES                  COLLECTION Nº001               CAT. Nº 001
BOOTCUT Nº01                  AFTER MIDNIGHT                 MADE IN ARGENTINA

SPOKEN — "These are Encore."
```
The riskiest finalist because it says "music" more plainly than the other four, but its ceiling is the highest on pure international reach — it needs no translation anywhere the brand might sell. Its real potential is temporal rather than geographic: a brand named for the moment nobody wants the night to end could eventually license that idea across more than clothing — a very small, very good encore of a name that keeps meaning the same thing at any scale.
